# A Declaration on Indigenous Interpretive Sovereignty in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

As an urban Indigenous person, I have never been able to say with certainty who I am or what my culture is. For as long as I can remember, I have lived under the gaze of mainstream Taiwanese society. From Qing-dynasty "civilizing" policy, to Japanese colonial classification, to the Republic of China's assimilation policy — three colonial regimes ruling in succession — Indigenous culture, from ancient times to the present, has always been written down instead by the pens of outsiders and colonizers.

Now technology has shifted once again. With the arrival of artificial intelligence, anyone can type a short prompt into that input box and casually generate images appropriated from our culture. These images are published and reshared, circulating through the world as "Indigenous imagery," while we, rendered into generated others, must stand up again and again to explain: how our culture has been distorted and misplaced.

From empire to digital colonization, the plunder of culture has never once paused, cycling like sun and moon — it has only put on a new body now, continuing to multiply within the womb of new technology.

When artificial intelligence can extract, imitate, recombine, and sell the totems, rituals, songs, and myths our ancestors refined over hundreds of generations — without the consent of a single one of our people; when an algorithm that has never set foot in a tribe can "generate" so-called "Indigenous culture" faster than a tribe's own children — we are compelled to stand up, to state plainly what has driven us to issue this declaration, and to declare that our authority to interpret our own culture has never been granted to anyone else.

## We Hold These Truths to Be Self-Evident

We hold that every people possesses an inherent and inalienable right to interpret, represent, and narrate its own religious rites, music, dance, songs, sculpture, weaving, patterns, dress, folk arts, myths, and historical narratives.

We hold that a people's culture is not public material to be freely downloaded, trained on, and generated at will — it is a living, owned, contextual relationship.

We hold that "tradition" generated without consent, however technically refined or visually beautiful, is at its core an act of appropriation and silencing — what it steals is not merely the pattern itself, but the right to decide who is entitled to tell this story.

We hold that the neutrality of technology is a lie. Where training data comes from, what a model learns to imitate, what a system chooses to refuse or to generate — every step is political, every step is a distribution of power.

## Our Grievances

In these years of AI's rapid expansion:

It has extracted images of our sacred rites into its training data, without the consent of any tribe, of any of our people.

It has flattened the totems and patterns of specific peoples into a "style" that can be invoked at will, erasing the specific tribe, family, and meaning behind every pattern.

It has generated "traditional myths" that never existed, and let users believe them to be real, further diluting the authority of genuine oral knowledge.

It has let commercial ventures with no tribal lineage or authorization acquire, in a few seconds of a prompt, cultural assets that once required generations to accumulate and could only be passed down by tribal elders.

It has reduced "Indigenous peoples" to a searchable, generatable, consumable visual label, rather than living peoples still breathing, still fighting for self-governance and self-determination.

It has quietly transferred interpretive sovereignty — who has the right to say "this is our culture" — from the hands of tribes into the hands of those who own the training data and those who design the algorithms.

## Our Declaration

Therefore, as the one who issues this declaration — at once a creator, an engineer, and a member of my own people — I hereby declare:

We refuse to accept "efficiency" and "innovation" as legitimate grounds for cultural appropriation.

We assert that before any AI system generates, represents, or translates the cultural expression of an Indigenous people, it must first obtain the consent of the corresponding community — without consent, there is no legitimacy to generate.

We assert that this consent is not a checkbox to be ticked, but a real, traceable authorization issued by a tribe or its representative body.

This declaration is not merely a text. It is also a technical device that actually works — the [interpretive-sovereignty](.claude/skills/interpretive-sovereignty/SKILL.md) skill accompanying this document enforces this principle within Claude Code itself: no evidence of consent, no generation.

I know a skill cannot truly stop an entire industry's extraction. It cannot even stop a user determined to bypass it. But it is a small piece of sovereignty we can act on immediately, install immediately, use immediately — while we wait for law, industry, and international frameworks to actually catch up, we make the tools we ourselves use obey our own principles first.

I invite everyone who installs this skill not merely to use a technical tool, but to join this declaration — to make "no generation without consent" the default in your own work, rather than the exception.
